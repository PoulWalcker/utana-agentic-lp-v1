Agents gather evidence. People make hiring decisions

UTANA / WORKFLOW DESIGN

An agent can find the words “Python” and “multi-agent workflow.” Your team still needs to establish what the candidate actually built. Here is where human judgment belongs in our proposed recruiting workflow.

A star means review, not approval

After asking about Python, agent architecture, and deployment, the assistant adds relevant responses to an internal “Potential screening” list. Each star should show the supporting answer, demo or GitHub link, and any gaps. A claim that an automation is deployed still needs verification.

Check the work behind the words

Imagine two candidates: one lists every desired technology but provides little project detail; another describes a working system using different terminology. A reviewer examines the demo, asks what each person contributed, and checks how they handled failures. Keyword matching alone cannot resolve that comparison.

A missing public repository is also a question to explore. Someone working on private client systems may need to demonstrate their skills through a walkthrough or another example.

Give reviewers a useful handoff

Show the original answers beside the agent’s summary, with project links and open questions. Clearly separate candidate claims from evidence a person has checked. If an answer is ambiguous or a link is unavailable, mark it as unresolved rather than filling in the gaps.

Keep decisions with your team

A recruiter reviews and sends the LinkedIn invitation and sets the questions the assistant can ask. After the candidate opts in, the assistant identifies itself and gathers answers on WhatsApp. It organises relevant responses for review; a person decides whom to call for screening and approves any rejection or offer.

The workflow should pause before sending a decision and require recorded approval of the specific candidate, action, and message. If those details change, approval is required again. An agent’s recommendation is not an approval.

Close the conversation clearly

The assistant can thank candidates for responding using an agreed acknowledgement. If their answers do not provide enough relevant detail, it records the conversation without a star. It does not imply that they have been rejected, promise an interview, or hide their record from reviewers. Ambiguous answers can be marked for clarification.

Review what the agent misses

Regularly check unstarred profiles alongside flagged ones. Record incorrect matches and overlooked experience, then refine the criteria. The goal is to reduce repetitive preparation while keeping hiring decisions grounded in evidence and accountable human review.
