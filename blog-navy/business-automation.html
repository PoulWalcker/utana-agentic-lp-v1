What does a more automated business look like?

UTANA / FIELD NOTES

Begin with work, not tools

A more automated business designs its processes to reduce repetitive work and unnecessary handoffs. The useful question is specific: which parts of this workflow need judgment, which need information, and which repeat predictably?

Start with one workflow

We design one workflow around a clear outcome, mapping its inputs, tasks, and human approval points. Once it works reliably, we can connect additional workflows and specialised agents, expanding from a tested foundation.

Measure before you automate

Establish a baseline before implementation: time per task, error rate, rework, and cost. Compare the same measures after launch to quantify time saved and mistakes reduced. Skipping this step makes improvements difficult to verify; a consistent before-and-after comparison is essential.

Expand what proves useful

Once the first workflow delivers measurable improvements, extend automation to other areas where it can reduce costs and free up capacity. Continuous improvement helps your business keep pace with technology and build a competitive advantage through faster, more reliable operations.
