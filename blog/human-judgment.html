From product brief to an approved email campaign

UTANA / WORKFLOW DESIGN

Imagine a software company launching a new reporting feature. The marketing team needs an email for existing customers, a clear explanation of the benefit, and a link to book a demo. This proposed workflow helps an agent prepare the campaign while people approve what customers receive.

Start with an agreed brief

The team provides the feature details, launch date, eligible customer group, brand guidelines, and destination page. The agent gathers these into a campaign brief and flags missing information before drafting. Product capabilities, prices, and offers come from approved material.

Prepare the email in your marketing software

The agent drafts subject-line options, preview text, a short message, and a call to action. It places the selected draft into an existing email template, checks the links, and prepares a preview for desktop and mobile. The handoff includes the draft, its source material, and any questions that need a decision.

Catch a convincing claim that goes too far

Suppose the draft says, “Cut your reporting time in half,” but the product brief only confirms that reports can be scheduled. The claim sounds persuasive, yet it has no supporting evidence. A reviewer replaces it with “Schedule your reports automatically” and confirms that the feature is available to the intended customers.

This is where human judgment matters: the agent can propose wording and flag inconsistencies, but the team is accountable for the promise it makes.

Review who will receive it

The agent prepares an audience preview using the team’s criteria, such as customers on an eligible plan who have opted in to product updates. It shows the filters and recipient count, applies unsubscribe exclusions, and flags missing or conflicting records. A person checks the audience before approving the campaign.

Approve the exact campaign before sending

The reviewer sees the final subject line, email, links, audience, and send time together. The campaign stays in draft until that version is approved. If the copy, recipient selection, or timing changes, it returns for review. The first pilot can keep the final send with a person using the marketing software.

Measure the work and the response

Record preparation and review time before the pilot, then compare the same work after introducing the agent. Track corrections, broken links caught, demo bookings, and unsubscribes. Use those results to improve the next campaign before extending the workflow to more audiences or channels.
