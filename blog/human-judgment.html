Turning campaign results into better enquiries

UTANA / WORKFLOW DESIGN

Utana wants qualified enquiries from businesses interested in workflow automation. In this proposed workflow, an agent helps review a Meta campaign, learn from the conversations it generates, and prepare changes for human approval.

Take people straight to a conversation

The ad appears on Facebook or Instagram with a button that opens a WhatsApp chat with Utana. The person sends a message, and our team can respond directly. Opening the chat alone does not count as an enquiry. This format is supported by ads that click to WhatsApp.

Agree on the outcome and spending limit

Before launch, the team approves the offer, audience, ads, total test budget, and campaign dates. It also defines a qualified enquiry: for example, someone representing a business, describing a specific workflow problem, and interested in discussing an automation project. The budget remains a test budget until results show what an enquiry costs.

Connect ad results with enquiry quality

The agent reviews campaign reports alongside a simple enquiry log maintained by the team. It tracks spending, conversations started, enquiries the team marks as qualified, and calls booked. Where a conversation can be linked to an ad, it records that source; otherwise it marks the source as unknown.

The main measure is cost per qualified enquiry: campaign spend divided by the number of qualified enquiries. Clicks and cheap conversations can help diagnose performance, but they do not establish whether the campaign is bringing useful business opportunities.

Turn a weak result into a specific test

Imagine the campaign attracts plenty of messages, but many are from people looking for AI courses. The agent summarises that pattern and proposes a change to the audience settings, explaining the evidence, the exact adjustment, and what it expects to improve. The team reviews and approves the change before it is applied.

If the next round brings business enquiries but few relevant projects, the agent investigates again. It might propose clearer ad copy about the workflows Utana automates or a more specific offer. Those changes also go to a person for approval.

Measure, adapt, and review again

Each approved change is logged with its date and the question being tested. The agent compares the next results with the previous period, allowing time for conversations and follow-up. It checks regularly, but waits for enough evidence before proposing another adjustment. A quiet day or a handful of replies is not enough to declare a winner.

The team can keep a useful change, reverse an unsuccessful one, or pause to rethink the campaign. Audience changes and budget increases require human approval. The cycle continues within the agreed spending limit; reaching the limit means stopping for review, not spending indefinitely to chase a target.

Keep the commercial decision with people

The agent makes campaign learning easier to organise and act on. Our team decides which enquiries are valuable and whether the results justify another test. Improvements are measured, not guaranteed; this workflow has not yet been validated in a live Utana campaign.
